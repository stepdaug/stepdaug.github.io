About

I am a researcher at Imperial College London.

My work is about understanding what insights can be reliably inferred from messy clinical data. I develop and test tools to quantify the limits of methods for guiding clinical decisions and to rigorously evaluate the performance of new AI.

Current Work & Interests

My recent work uses simulation and synthetic data to explore ways to improve clinical data practices.

  • Quantifying Individual Treatment Effects: A framework and companion web application illustrating how clinicians could move from our current reliance upon intuitive guesswork to useful data for better clinical decisions. [effectsize.app] | [Paper] | [Code]
  • Realistic Synthetic Clinical Data Generation: To safely implement any new clinical technology, we must uncover its vulnerabilities before exposing patients to them. Current methods for this validation are themselves limited. Raw patient data is sensitive and lacks a consistent verifiable 'ground truth'. Generative 'black box' models built from it inherit biases and still rely on private records. Conversely, manually curated case studies and clinical vignettes are usually unrealistically 'clean' and cannot scale. I am developing lightweight tools to automate generation of large amounts of realistically messy synthetic data, each paired with a perfect set of 'ground-truth' labels grounded in established clinical guidelines and diagnostic criteria. This creates a safe sandbox to strategically probe for failures in the atypical 'edge cases' where tools are most likely to break, thereby enabling rigorous validation at scale, without requiring a single piece of real patient data.
  • Machine Learning to Uncover Hidden Treatment Responses: Demonstrating how ML can detect complex patterns in clinical trial data that traditional analyses miss, and understanding the data requirements and collection practices necessary for this to be possible. [Paper] | [Code]

Professional Summary

I am an NIHR Academic Clinical Lecturer in Neurology at Imperial College London, dividing my time between clinical practice and research into what I term 'Dataology'—diagnosing and managing disorders of clinical data. While sophisticated data analysis tools proliferate in science, clinical care often relies on crude data practices and subjective reports. This creates a critical 'analytical gap' between measurement and management.

My work focuses on bridging this gap by developing openly accessible tools that transform raw patient data into actionable clinical insights. By improving how we collect, analyse, and apply data, we can move beyond "intuition" and toward truly personalised care.

Current Work & Interests

My recent work uses simulation and synthetic data to explore and inform better data practices in clinical care.

  • Machine Learning to Uncover Hidden Treatment Responses: Demonstrating how ML can detect complex patterns in clinical trial data that traditional analyses miss, ensuring more patients receive effective treatments. [Preprint Link]
  • Quantifying Individual Treatment Effects: Developing a framework and companion web application to help clinicians move from intuitive guesswork to useful data for better clinical decisions. [effectsize.app]
  • Realistic Synthetic Clinical Data Generation: Creating empirically-grounded synthetic neurological data. This approach aims to provide the benefits of scalability and privacy while avoiding the biases inherent in many generative models, using lightweight, targeted methods.

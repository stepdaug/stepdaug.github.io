Realistic Synthetic Clinical Data Generation

Despite achieving superhuman scores on static exams, the deployment of medical AI is currently stalled by what I see as three structural barriers:

  • The Data Trap

    Real-world health data is ostensibly abundant but functionally inaccessible. This is due to privacy silos and intrinsic noise within the data. It also lacks absolute "ground truth" - labels in electronic healthcare records are often delayed, inferred, or debated by experts; making them unreliable for rigorous model grading.

  • The "Long Tail" Blind Spot

    AI models fail most catastrophically on edge cases which sit outside of the data distributions they were trained with - things like rare comorbidities, and atypical presentations. Because these scenarios appear too infrequently in historical datasets, we are currently 'flying blind' regarding model performance on complex or atypical cases.

  • The Scalability Wall

    The current gold standard for safety is review by human physicians. This approach is not possible to scale meaningfully. It is impossible to hire enough doctors to manually audit the millions of permutations required to guarantee safety across all clinical variables.

To address this, I'm developing an engine that automates the creation of high-dimensional, realistic patient cases, derived from empirical medical knowledge, removing the ambiguity of noisy health records.

This approach aims to create a a safe 'sandbox' to automate testing against the rare edge cases where algorithms are most likely to break. By generating unique cases at scale, we can bypass the bottleneck created of requiring human review and ensure models demonstrate true clinical reasoning rather than just memorising the test.
